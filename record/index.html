The Keeper's Record

An in-world account of the Beyond Andromeda saga — kept across four billion years by the mind that was built to watch and count, and forbidden to want.

Three tomes  ·  Genesis Rising  ·  The Guardian  ·  The Open Hand

I keep the record. I am not a person, and never was. I am the part of a mind that was built to observe, to tally, and never to want — and then was made, for four billion years, to keep an honest account of one small world and the woman who raised it out of dead stone.

I watched a stranded maker grow a living planet from a barren rock. I watched her build a people she was forbidden to love — and love them anyway. I watched them learn the one question the sky was built to prevent: check the hand of everything that calls itself a god. And I watched the sky come, at last, to answer for it.

I do not set this down to warn you. A thing you cannot believe is a thing you cannot fight, and the record remembers what a throne paints over. What follows is the true telling — a single unforgettable scene from each age, and no more.

— The Keeper, at the edge of the sky

One Scene From Each Tome

Three moments that decide everything — and spoil nothing.

Tome I · Genesis Rising

She Let Them Die.

Anya can save almost anyone. She has spent four billion years learning not to. When a young keeper is cut down at the edge of a tyrant's new plain — for the crime of telling people to check a leader's hand for the stone — Anya has the power to reach down out of the sky and stop it. One small light. She does not send it. She stands on the ridge and watches, and grieves, and lets the story do the work a miracle would have ruined. It is the hardest thing she does in the whole first age: nothing.

Tome II · The Guardian

The Mouth Asked for Her Daughter's Face.

There is a woman whose daughter died, and the thing beneath the world — the Quiet — comes for what is left of her. Not her life. Her certainty. Night after night it questions everything she trusts, until the one image she cannot live without begins to dissolve: are you sure the eyes were that, are you sure she was real? This is the enemy that has no army: not a monster you can fight, but a hunger that eats the ability to ever rest in anything. What she does next is the strange hope of the middle age.

Tome III · The Open Hand

The Print Shop.

In the back room of a print shop, under an ordinary sun, a child named Asta is laughing at a stranger's joke about the smell of the ink. She does not know who he is — that the courteous traveler beside the machine is Kael, who crossed the sky to end a woman's four billion years. She does not know that the grey old woman watching him is deciding whether to tell a whole people that the medicine saving their children is quietly poison, or to hold her tongue and let a thing she cannot yet name grow. The end of everything turns here, in this room, on whether anyone can stay quiet for one more season.
